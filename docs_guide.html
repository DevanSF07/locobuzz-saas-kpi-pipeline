Chapter 1: The 6 Pipeline Execution Steps

This chapter walks chronologically through every stage of the automated data pipeline engineering and analytical engine deployed at LocoBuzz Solutions.

The Core Objective: Build a scalable, automated pipeline that ingests over 97,000 raw monthly B2B transaction and product telemetry logs, calculates complex SaaS recurring revenue movements, surfaces retention bottlenecks through cohort heatmaps, and validates business interventions through rigorous statistical testing.

Step 1: Synthetic Enterprise Data Generation (17K+ Accounts)

Enterprise B2B SaaS analytics requires realistic volume, distributions, and behavioral signals. The data generator (pipeline/generate_synthetic_data.py) simulates a 12-month operating history across 17,250 client accounts:

  • Account Stratification: 45% Starter ($299/mo), 40% Growth ($999/mo), 15% Enterprise ($3,499+/mo).
  • Product Telemetry: Monthly logs for AI sentiment tokens consumed, API calls, and resolved CX tickets.
  • Injected Business Anomaly: Accounts in the Growth tier crossing their 100,000 token quota face an uncompetitive overage markup ($0.0120 vs $0.0100/1K tokens), triggering Month 3–5 invoice disputes and churn.

Step 2: Automated Postgres SQL ETL Pipeline

The raw CSV files are ingested and normalized into production-grade dimensional models (sql/01_schema_ddl.sql and sql/02_etl_transformations.sql). The pipeline enforces foreign key integrity, indexes on lookup keys, and automated validation assertions.

PostgreSQL DDL Star-Schema Overview

    account_id VARCHAR(32) PRIMARY KEY,
    company_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    industry VARCHAR(100),
    region VARCHAR(100),

-- Monthly Billing & Telemetry Fact Table
CREATE TABLE fct_monthly_billing (
    billing_month VARCHAR(7) NOT NULL,
    account_id VARCHAR(32) NOT NULL REFERENCES dim_accounts(account_id),
    tenure_month INTEGER NOT NULL,
    plan_tier VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    mrr_amount NUMERIC(10, 2) NOT NULL,
    overage_amount NUMERIC(10, 2) NOT NULL,
    tokens_consumed BIGINT,
    previous_mrr NUMERIC(10, 2) DEFAULT 0.00,
    mrr_change NUMERIC(10, 2) DEFAULT 0.00,
    revenue_category VARCHAR(30), -- 'NEW', 'EXPANSION', 'CONTRACTION', 'RETAINED'
    PRIMARY KEY (billing_month, account_id)
);

Step 3: Revenue Categorization via SQL Window Functions

Tracking SaaS revenue movements requires comparing each customer's current billing against their previous month. The pipeline uses the Postgres LAG() window function to dynamically classify revenue changes into standardized categories: 

LAG(t.total_billed, 1, 0.0) OVER (
    PARTITION BY t.account_id 
    ORDER BY t.billing_month
) AS previous_mrr,

CASE 
    WHEN tenure_month = 0 THEN 'NEW'
    WHEN mrr_amount > previous_mrr THEN 'EXPANSION'
    WHEN mrr_amount < previous_mrr THEN 'CONTRACTION'
    ELSE 'RETAINED'
END AS revenue_category

Step 4: AARRR Cohort Retention Heatmap Matrix

Accounts are grouped into cohorts by their signup month ($C_0, C_1, \dots, C_{11}$) and tracked across tenure intervals (Month 0 to Month 11). The retention rate measures the percentage of starting accounts that remain active and paying:

Retention Rate(Cohort, Month t) = Active Accounts(Cohort, t) / Initial Starting Accounts(Cohort)

The aggregated table (kpi_monthly_cohorts) is outputted as a triangular matrix, exposing where accounts drop off. The analysis revealed that while Month 1 retention was 95.1%, it deteriorated to 83.7% by Month 4—specifically driven by Mid-Market Growth tier accounts experiencing bill shock.

Chapter 2: Masterclass on Techniques Used

Technique 1: Dimensional Data Modeling (Star Schema)

In production SaaS analytics, raw transactional logs should never be queried directly by executive dashboards. We separated entities into:

  • Dimension Tables (dim_accounts): Contains slow-changing descriptive attributes (Company Name, Industry, Region, Plan Tier, CAC, Experiment Group). Grain: One row per customer account.
  • Fact Tables (fct_monthly_billing): Contains additive and semi-additive business metrics (MRR, Base Fee, Overage Charges, Discounts, Tokens Consumed). Grain: One row per account per billing month.
  • Aggregate Reporting Tables (kpi_monthly_cohorts): Pre-aggregated tables indexed by cohort and tenure, optimizing dashboard query times from seconds to under 10 milliseconds.

Technique 2: Advanced SQL Window Functions

Window functions perform calculations across a set of table rows related to the current row without collapsing rows like a standard GROUP BY.

FUNCTION() OVER (PARTITION BY partition_column ORDER BY sort_column [ROWS BETWEEN ...])
  • PARTITION BY account_id: Isolates calculations within each customer's specific history.
  • ORDER BY billing_month: Ensures sequential chronological processing.
  • LAG(value, offset, default): Inspects preceding month billing to determine upgrade, downgrade, or steady retention status.

Technique 4: The AARRR (Pirate Metrics) Framework

Developed by Dave McClure, the AARRR framework tracks customer progression through the product lifecycle:

  • Acquisition: Lead generation and customer account creation. Tracked by Plan Tier, Region, and Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).
  • Activation: First value realization. For LocoBuzz, defined as setting up brand listening streams and consuming first 20,000 sentiment analysis tokens in Month 1.
  • Retention: Repeated platform usage and billing renewals month over month. Visualized via the 12-month cohort retention heatmap.
  • Revenue: Expanding Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) through seat expansion and additional AI token bundles.
  • Referral / Advocacy: Account upgrades and multi-department expansion (e.g. CX adding Marketing listening seats).

Technique 5: Root-Cause Forensic Analysis & Basis Points

What is a Basis Point (bps)?
One basis point equals 1/100th of 1% (0.01% or 0.0001). In pricing, margin analysis, and take rates, basis points express minute per-unit cost differences that compound into massive revenue impact across millions of transactions.

The Forensic Discovery: LocoBuzz Growth accounts paid $999 for 100,000 tokens ($0.00999 ≈ $0.0100 per 1K). But when an account crossed 100,000 tokens, the legacy billing engine charged $0.0120 per 1K tokens. That difference is:

$0.0120 - $0.0100 = $0.0020 per 1,000 tokens = 20.0 Basis Points (0.20%)

An account consuming 250,000 tokens faced $1,800 in overages on top of their $999 base fee—an invoice jumping by 180%! Because Enterprise accounts negotiated overages down to $0.0040/1K tokens (-30 bps), Mid-Market clients felt penalized as they grew, precipitating churn.

Technique 6: Rigorous A/B Testing & Statistical Inference

To ensure the churn reduction was not a seasonal fluke or random noise, we applied two classical statistical tests:

A. Two-Proportion Pooled Z-Test

Z = (p_control - p_variant) / sqrt(p_pool * (1 - p_pool) * (1/N_control + 1/N_variant))

With $p_{control} = 0.2277$, $p_{variant} = 0.1720$, $N_{ctrl} = 3,465$, and $N_{var} = 3,430$, the test yielded $Z = 5.78$, corresponding to a two-tailed p-value of $7.44 \times 10^{-9}$ ($p \ll 0.001$).

B. Chi-Square Contingency Test of Independence (Χ²)

Testing independence between treatment group and churn status with 1 degree of freedom:

Χ² = ∑ (Observed - Expected)^2 / Expected = 33.07 (p = 8.89 x 10^-9)

Both tests reject the null hypothesis with > 99.99% confidence, proving the pricing adjustment caused the churn reduction.
